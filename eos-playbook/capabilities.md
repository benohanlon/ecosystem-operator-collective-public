# Capabilities and services

A **capability** is something an ecosystem function can do repeatedly. A **service** puts a capability to work for a team. A [package](packages.md) combines services around an outcome a team values.

## Core capabilities

| Capability | What it helps teams do | Example services |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ecosystem narrative, storytelling and confidence | Explain what the ecosystem is building and earn trust through consistent communication. | Ecosystem communications, technical blogs and content |
| Technology adoption, developer enablement and coalitions | Help builders adopt technology and bring partners together around work they can do jointly. | Developer relations, developer education, coalitions and alliances |
| Ecosystem intelligence, segmentation and sensemaking | Understand who participates, how they relate and what their activity means for decisions. | Ecosystem mapping and segmentation, ecosystem intelligence and insights |
| Governance and treasury coordination | Help participants understand proposals and coordinate around governance and funding decisions. | Governance activity monitoring, representation in shared governance bodies |
| Delegate engagement and relationships | Build relationships with delegates so teams can hear concerns and discuss decisions over time. | Delegate engagement, network making and relationship building |
| Network operator engagement and resilience | Help operators adopt changes and keep the network running reliably. | Validator and operator engagement, technical documentation |
| Venture capability and revenue | Help ventures draw on shared expertise, find partners and reach users. | Centre of excellence, ecosystem engagement strategy and planning |
| Regional activation and representation | Build relationships and participation in regions that matter to the ecosystem. | Ecosystem mapping and segmentation, community events |
| Reputation, risk and rapid response | Spot emerging issues and coordinate a response when trust or adoption is at risk. | Ecosystem intelligence and insights, rapid response |

The service catalogue below contains 30 core services and six additional candidates for assessment. A service can contribute to more than one capability.

## Diagnose and measure progress

| Service | What it does and why |
| --- | --- |
| Ecosystem dashboards | Give teams a shared view of outcomes and where progress stalls. |
| Venture metrics framework | Proposed framework for comparing measures across ventures where they track the same outcome. |
| Ecosystem mapping and segmentation | Map participants, relationships and routes to the people you want to reach. |
| Ecosystem intelligence and insights | Bring recurring questions and barriers to the people who can act and report back. |
| Ecosystem data | Establish a baseline with product, network, developer or community data. |
| Ecosystem diagnostics and intervention | Identify where a team loses progress and choose a plausible cause to test. |
| Ecosystem analysis | Compare other ecosystems for audiences, partnership routes and gaps. |
| Technical intelligence | Assess complementary or competing technologies, standards and protocols. |

## Help developers discover, start and build

| Service | What it does and why |
| --- | --- |
| Developer marketing | Show developers what they can build and why they should try it, using channels they trust. |
| Developer advocacy and experience | Help developers get past barriers and take recurring issues back to product or engineering. |
| Developer relations | Build continuing relationships with developer teams and connect them to expertise and pilot opportunities. |
| Technical documentation | Maintain instructions and examples people can use independently. |
| Technical blogs and content | Explain how a technology works and guide readers from announcements to practical examples. |
| Onboarding | Build a working path to the first useful action and ask early users or partners to try it. |
| Developer activity monitoring | Follow contribution and usage across repositories, tools and integrations. |
| Developer education | Build training around the skills people need to do useful work with the product. |

## Keep communities connected and able to act

| Service | What it does and why |
| --- | --- |
| Ecosystem communications | Explain changes and next steps with consistent messaging across channels. |
| Community engagement and experience | Help participants find useful work and get help from people who can act. |
| Community channel management and operations | Run channels so participants can find answers and get issues to an owner. |
| Strategic facilitation and moderation | Bring the right people together to make a decision or remove a blocker. |
| Community events | Run workshops, meetups or hackathons around learning, contribution or joint work. |
| Ecosystem engagement strategy and planning | Agree whom to engage, what they need and how participation should advance an outcome. |
| Centre of excellence | Help teams learn proven practices through coaching, examples and peer support. |

| Service | What it does and why |
| --- | --- |

## Support network participation and governance

| Service | What it does and why |
| --- | --- |

## Additional services for assessment

Assess these options for fit and delivery capacity before including them in a package.

| Service | What it does and why |
| --- | --- |
| AI ecosystem agents | Use agents for routine information gathering, user questions or governance support. |
| CRM strategy and management | Maintain a shared record of relationships, commitments and feedback across teams. |
| Ecosystem incubation and acceleration | Help projects reach milestones with funding, mentoring, introductions or technical support. |
| Tokenomics and incentives | Assess or design how rewards and costs shape participation and network health. |
| Governance as a service | Help a team put usable proposal, decision, delegation and reporting processes in place. |
| Commercial partnerships and growth | Scope joint integration or distribution work with clear commitments and economics. |

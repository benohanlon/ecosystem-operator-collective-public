# Collective case template

Each case explains how we addressed a growth, adoption or distribution problem through ecosystem building and developer marketing. Pick one problem a reader can recognise and one episode that shows how the work changed it.

**Choose the principle the case will demonstrate. Then tell the story: constraint → change in how people work together → what each side brings and gains → what becomes possible.**

This is a pattern drawn from the examples in *Ecosystem Edge*. The book uses its cases to explain a principle introduced in the surrounding chapter. Nokia's rejection shows how bringing the buyer into the design process helped ARM and Texas Instruments find value they had missed. Rolls-Royce's need for trained technicians shows how a shared roadmap gave employers, schools and government something they could each invest in. The Guardian opens with its reach and works back to the decision to let outside sites use and distribute its content.

Choose the detail that explains why the arrangement worked. For a DAO, the principle is that legitimacy can emerge when people have a meaningful part in shaping, debating and approving the rules. Workshops gave participants a way to influence the draft. The convention let their delegates work through differences. Ratification records formal consent. Ongoing governance spaces let representatives deliberate before exercising that authority. Each belongs only where it explains a different part of the case.

Choose the opening that sets up the principle: a rejected proposal, a concrete constraint or a result that needs explaining.

## Copy-ready format

~~~markdown
## [Title the problem the reader recognises]

[Open with a specific constraint, setback, decision or surprising result. Name the project and what was at stake. Show who held the knowledge, capability, trust or reach the team needed.]

[Describe what changed in the relationship. Show what each essential party could contribute, why it had a reason to do so and what they could now achieve together. Use a concrete detail that makes the arrangement intelligible.]

[Show what became possible and give an observation that supports it. Use the opening or closing to make clear what principle the case demonstrates. Keep figures that establish the stakes or tell the reader whether the arrangement worked.]

[Link to the strongest evidence for the case.]
~~~

Use short narrative paragraphs. Add a paragraph when the story needs a second turn to make the sequence clear. Lead with a number when it sets the stakes or poses a question the case can answer. A result can also be a product someone shipped, a decision others could finally make, or a commitment a partner acted on.

## Check before publishing

- **Opening.** Can someone outside the project see the problem and why it mattered in the first paragraph?
- **Decision.** Can you point to the choice or action that made it possible for people to work together? If you list activities, does each one help explain what changed?
- **Participants.** Can you say what each essential participant contributed and what gave them a reason to act? Is the team's part clear?
- **Result.** Can you point to what changed for the people in the story? Does the result answer the problem the case opened with?
- **Evidence.** Does the source support the wording, the timeframe and who did the work? Separate the result of a specific intervention from wider ecosystem growth. Label a proposed method or an illustration as such.

If any answer is missing, keep the case in draft and write down the specific event, decision or result you need to verify.

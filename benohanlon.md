## How I work

I get hands-on first, then build the team around what works. At Input Output, I started as an individual contributor and built the ecosystem function into a team of 5–10 as the company became a venture studio. We built product marketing capability and worked with independently led business units on positioning, adoption and fundraising through the treasury.

I think about growth as a Celtic knot. Product, distribution, adoption, partnerships and community affect one another. I look at those relationships to decide where an intervention will make the greatest difference. That’s why numbers come before strategy. A small set of system-wide units of account gives teams and partners a shared axis around which to orient, allowing high alignment and loose coupling. They can choose their own strategies and see whether their work adds up to progress for the whole. It’s how I avoid “activity therapy”, where teams optimise locally and the system as a whole makes little progress. [EOS Playbook](eos-playbook/README.md) · [Numbers Come Before Strategy](writing/numbers-come-before-strategy.md)

## The commercial case for open source

We built our customer, who then chose to pay us. The Cardano community took stewardship of a $1.3B treasury. Input Output had to make its case to the people governing the network, and I led the campaign that won $190M in continuity funding for research and engineering. A forum I’d built about a year earlier gave voting representatives a place to deliberate and gave us a way to understand their concerns.

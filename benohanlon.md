## Origin story

I started in privacy protocols in the Zcash lineage. When we built Cardano as an open financial operating system, I built the operator community, ran Input Output’s delegation programme and brought together the engineers expanding network capacity and the developers building on it. Independent pools took over block production. By September 2022, more than 3,000 pools were operating. [The cold-start case](https://benohanlon.md/cases/README.md#solving-the-cold-start-problem)

Moving governance onchain meant agreeing a constitution, standing up Intersect as a member-based organisation and running workshops where people could shape the rules. I led Input Output’s ecosystem work on the constitution as part of a wider effort across several organisations. I built the DRep Forum ahead of the first full treasury funding round. Once the community took stewardship of the treasury, I led our campaign for continued research and engineering. **We built our customer, who then chose to pay us.** [The governance case](https://benohanlon.md/cases/README.md#scaling-governance)

Then I built on that same network at RealFi. I designed and launched the Stake Pool Operator Accelerator so operators could bring people who already trusted them into the testnet for stablecoin and real-world asset infrastructure backed by private credit. Those early users showed us where they got stuck as we prepared for mainnet. [The RealFi case](https://benohanlon.md/cases/README.md#reaching-early-users-through-communities-they-already-trust)

## Cases

### Reaching early users through communities they already trust

*Asset-based community development*

RealFi was preparing to launch USDrf, a stablecoin that gives holders access to returns from real-world credit when they stake it. We needed people to try it on Cardano and tell us where they got stuck. I designed and launched an accelerator around stake pool operators who already knew how to reach and help those users. Operators earned points tied to what the people they brought in earned on the testnet. PetLoverStake turned that opportunity into a walkthrough for its own community. RealFi’s Head of Product credited testers, bug reporters and the operators who brought their delegators with helping the mainnet launch go smoothly. [Read the case](https://benohanlon.md/cases/README.md#reaching-early-users-through-communities-they-already-trust)

### Activating developers

*Developer marketing & education*

After Mary added native assets to Cardano, Alonzo was set to bring smart contracts to mainnet. Developers had to decide whether learning Plutus was worth their time. Input Output’s education team gave them early access and training, and the community and developer relations function I led supported the programme. The 2021 Summit Capstone gave developers a reason to put that learning to work, with engineers from IO and partner companies mentoring teams on problems other builders faced. Teams submitted 16 projects, including a toolkit for testing Cardano integrations. Four developers from the first two Pioneer cohorts later contributed to Plutus Community Docs, giving the next cohort a place to start. [Read the case](https://benohanlon.md/cases/README.md#activating-developers)

### Unblocking developers

*Developer relations & product feedback*

After Alonzo brought Plutus smart contracts to Cardano mainnet, application teams knew which issues stopped them from shipping. The engineers maintaining Plutus knew what it would take to fix them. I started DApp Orbit to bring those perspectives together. Our developer relations team gathered evidence from builders and met with engineering each week to decide which issues to tackle first. The team’s March 2023 retrospective records that the active backlog fell from more than 200 issues to 46, with issues that had taken months being resolved in days. [Read the case](https://benohanlon.md/cases/README.md#unblocking-developers)

### Solving the cold-start problem

*Network growth & operator adoption*

Cardano’s Shelley upgrade moved block production towards independent stake pools. Cardano’s aim of opening up access to finance and its commitment to peer-reviewed research gave operators reasons to help build it. Running a pool gave them a business they could build, but they still had to invest before they knew whether enough people would delegate to make it viable. Stake Pool School taught them how to get a pool running. I designed and ran Input Output’s delegation programme, which selected 100 of 299 applicants in its first round and committed 3.2 million ada per pool in temporary delegation. CO2-Pool described how it planned to use that window to build support of its own. By the end of March 2021, community pools were producing every new block on Cardano. [Read the case](https://benohanlon.md/cases/README.md#solving-the-cold-start-problem)

### Getting a new network ready through an existing ecosystem

*Product go-to-market & partner distribution*

Midnight, a privacy-focused partner chain to Cardano, needed experienced operators to test block production before mainnet. Cardano stake pool operators already had that expertise. Midnight offered them a chance to put it to work on a new network and help improve the experience for others. I coordinated early DevNet support, and our ecosystem team later helped recruit Cardano operators for Midnight testnet-02. The team’s 2025 retrospective records 137 valid block producer candidate registrations from Cardano stake pool operators. A community-written guide shows operators passing on what they learned. [Read the case](https://benohanlon.md/cases/README.md#getting-a-new-network-ready-through-an-existing-ecosystem)

### Building a coalition to fund shared infrastructure

*Partnerships & coalitions*

In Cardano’s first full on-chain funding cycle, independent infrastructure builders needed decisions in time to retain staff and plan delivery. Voting representatives needed to know what they were funding and how it would be checked. CDEC, the Cardano Development & Ecosystem Cooperative, shifted from pursuing a formal consortium to helping builders coordinate proposals. Rupert Whitehead ran coalition calls and membership. I led an operating review and published our open letter, explaining the costs of delay and how milestone-based payments would protect the treasury. The team’s 2025 retrospective records that CDEC supported 42 proposals and that 26 of the 27 it shortlisted were approved. [Read the case](https://benohanlon.md/cases/README.md#building-a-coalition-to-fund-shared-infrastructure)

### Coordinating teams and partners with different priorities

*Ecosystem strategy & orchestration*

At Input Output’s venture studio, each venture had its own priorities. I built and led a shared ecosystem function that could bring in people and expertise when they served a venture’s goals. Blockfrost wanted its Cardano API to rely less on central infrastructure. Stake pool operators wanted a new source of revenue from infrastructure they already ran. Our team brought the operator relationships, and Blockfrost provided the software and routed developer traffic. More than 100 operators took part and demonstrated that the model could serve real traffic. Blockfrost closed the programme after the funding model for the next stage failed to gain approval. [Read the case](https://benohanlon.md/cases/README.md#coordinating-teams-and-partners-with-different-priorities)

### Scaling governance

*Stakeholder engagement*

As Cardano moved to full on-chain governance, delegated representatives (DReps) needed a place to compare what they heard from constituents and scrutinise teams seeking treasury funding. I built the DRep Forum ahead of the first full funding round and used it in the continuity campaign I led. Esther Eze and I published a briefing that set out arguments on both sides and explained the difference between voting to advance a budget and voting to release funds. In August 2025, the community approved ₳96.8 million for Input Output Engineering’s roadmap with 73.93% support. The people governing the network had become the people deciding whether to pay for its core development. [Read the case](https://benohanlon.md/cases/README.md#scaling-governance)

### Giving developers a way to shape shared standards

*Open standards & ecosystem coordination*

As Cardano prepared for Shelley in 2020, contributors needed a public way to propose technical changes and get feedback from people who would use them. I worked with Frederic Johnson on contributor guidance for Cardano Improvement Proposals and invited public feedback as we introduced the process. The process gave people a shared way to state a problem, put forward a design and invite technical review. A later proposal, CIP-31, became part of the Vasil upgrade. It let applications read information on the blockchain without spending the transaction output that held it. [Read the case](https://benohanlon.md/cases/README.md#giving-developers-a-way-to-shape-shared-standards)

## Ecosystem Operating System Playbook

I use the Ecosystem Operating System (EOS) to coordinate work at scale across autonomous teams inside and outside a company.

### Numbers come before strategy

I start by establishing what would benefit the ecosystem as a whole and expressing it in a few system-wide units of account. These measures make the winning conditions visible and give teams a shared basis for coordinating without direct authority. Each team still owns its strategy and the measures it can influence.

### Interface for impact

Each team sees a different part of the same system. A decision can make sense for one team and still work against what the ecosystem needs as a whole. I go to teams to understand their work as they see it, what they value, what shapes their decisions and what they count as progress. The team chooses which capabilities it wants to draw on, whether they come from partners, community, developer relations or AI. Together we combine the relevant services into a package that helps the team achieve an outcome it values and advances the ecosystem’s shared goals. The team can judge what the work delivered using the measures it already uses.
